# Apple Ripening Behavior

Apples are climacteric fruit, but unlike banana they are typically harvested at
a mature, preclimacteric stage specifically so that ripening can be delayed
substantially through cold storage - this is what gives apples their unusually
long storage potential compared to most other fresh produce.

Increasingly, gradual cooling (rather than rapid cooling immediately after
harvest) is recommended for many apple cultivars, especially chilling-sensitive
ones, since very fast temperature drops can themselves induce disorders.

Controlled-atmosphere (CA) storage, which lowers oxygen and raises CO2 relative
to normal air, further slows ripening beyond what temperature alone achieves,
which is why CA storage life significantly exceeds standard refrigerated air
storage life for the same cultivar.
