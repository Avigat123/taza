# Apple Spoilage Indicators

## Storage scald
A physiological disorder causing skin browning, particularly common in
cultivars like Granny Smith grown in hot, dry climates. Not caused by
pathogens - a preventable storage-condition disorder rather than decay.

## Fungal decay
The dominant fungal decay organisms in stored/exported apples are typically
Penicillium expansum (blue mould) and Botrytis cinerea (grey mould). Both
develop most readily at sites of physical damage - bruising, stem puncture,
rough harvest handling, or packing-line damage. This means visible bruising or
wounds on a batch are a meaningful risk signal independent of temperature and
humidity being otherwise correct.

## Chilling/low-temperature injury
Some cultivars, even at recommended near-0°C storage, can show low-temperature
injury in some growing seasons - implying that "correct" storage temperature is
not a strict guarantee against all spoilage, particularly across cultivars or
seasons not explicitly known to the system.

## General framing
Apple shelf life is not a single fixed number - it is a function of cultivar,
harvest maturity, storage history/regime, firmness at the time of assessment,
ethylene exposure, and cold-chain discipline, not storage time alone.
