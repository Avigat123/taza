# Banana Post-Harvest Handling

Precooling (rapid cooling to storage temperature immediately after harvest) is
critical - banana is typically precooled using cold air (room cooling or forced
air cooling) rather than water, since water contact is avoided to reduce decay
risk. Delayed precooling shortens overall shelf life even if later storage
conditions are correct, because the fruit spends more time at ambient
respiration rates before slowing down.

Since harvested bananas cannot replenish moisture lost through the peel,
low-humidity storage or transport causes shriveling and visible weight loss -
this is a one-way, cumulative loss, not something that recovers if humidity is
later corrected.

Transport/storage duration interacts with temperature: brief exposure to
elevated temperature during transport can be enough to trigger the climacteric
(ripening) stage even if the bulk of the journey was properly refrigerated -
i.e., cold-chain breaks are not "averaged out," a short warm excursion can have
an outsized effect.

Rough handling during loading/unloading creates wounds in the peel that serve
as entry points for decay-causing microorganisms, independent of temperature
and humidity control.
