# Banana Spoilage Indicators and Chilling Injury

## Chilling injury
Bananas are highly sensitive to chilling injury below approximately 12-13°C,
with sensitivity depending on cultivar, maturity stage, and how long the
exposure lasts (lower temperature causes injury faster). Symptoms include:
- Peel browning, dull or "smoky" surface discoloration
- Dark-brown streaks in the sub-epidermal (under-skin) tissue
- Abnormal ripening or, in severe cases, complete failure to ripen
- Increased sensitivity to physical/mechanical damage after chilling

Because chilling injury symptoms can take time to appear, a banana batch that
was briefly exposed to sub-12°C temperatures may still look visually acceptable
at the time of imaging but be on a trajectory toward reduced shelf life.

## Physical damage and decay
Skin abrasions (from fruit rubbing against packaging, other fruit, or handling
equipment) accelerate water loss and browning when combined with low humidity.
Cigar-end rot (caused by Verticillium theobromae / Trachysphaera fructigena)
appears as a dry, ash-like rot at the finger tip and is worsened by bruising
and delayed cooling.

## Compatibility warning
Bananas should not be stored or transported alongside chilling-resistant crops,
or crops requiring low humidity (e.g. onions, garlic, winter squash), since the
humidity/temperature bananas need conflicts with those commodities' needs.
