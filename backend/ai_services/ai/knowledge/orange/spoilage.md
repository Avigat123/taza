# Orange/Citrus Spoilage Indicators

## Chilling injury
Symptoms include pitting and brown discoloration of the peel, with increased
susceptibility to subsequent decay. Severity increases with longer exposure to
low temperature (below roughly 5°C for mandarin-type citrus; oranges generally
tolerate somewhat lower temperatures than mandarins before injury appears, but
exact thresholds are cultivar-dependent).

## Oil spotting (oleocellosis)
Caused by physical handling of turgid (fully hydrated) fruit that ruptures oil
cells in the peel, releasing oil that damages surrounding rind tissue. This is
a handling-damage indicator rather than a storage-condition indicator.

## Aging/shriveling
Symptoms include peel shriveling and injury concentrated around the stem end,
generally associated with prolonged storage duration and/or moisture loss over
time rather than an acute temperature or humidity failure.

## Quality maintenance
Freedom from physical damage (abrasion, bruising), skin blemishes and
discoloration, freezing damage, chilling injury, and insect damage are the
primary visual quality criteria referenced in postharvest guidance for citrus.
