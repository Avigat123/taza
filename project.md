# Taza 🌱

## AI-Powered Fresh Produce Intelligence Platform

> **Predict freshness. Prevent waste. Save value.**

Taza is an AI-powered platform designed to reduce avoidable waste in India's fresh-produce supply chain.

It combines computer vision, quality parameters, storage conditions, supply-chain data, predictive machine learning, and intelligent decision-making to help businesses understand the condition of produce and determine what action should be taken before it becomes waste.

---

# 1. Problem

India's fresh-produce supply chain involves multiple stages:

Farm → Aggregator → Pack House → Mandi → Warehouse → Cold Chain → Retailer → Consumer

At each stage, decisions around quality, inventory, transportation, and demand can be difficult because information is fragmented and produce quality changes continuously.

The hackathon problem statement estimates that India loses approximately **15–30% of fruits and vegetables before they reach consumers**.

Major challenges include:

- Uncertain produce quality
- Manual quality inspection
- Lack of freshness visibility
- Spoilage during transportation
- Poor inventory prioritization
- Temperature and storage issues
- Demand uncertainty
- Limited visibility across the supply chain

Taza focuses on turning available produce and supply-chain data into actionable intelligence.

---

# 2. Solution

Taza provides a centralized intelligence layer for fresh-produce operations.

The system combines:

- Computer vision
- Produce quality parameters
- Harvest information
- Storage conditions
- Supply-chain information
- Inventory data
- Demand information
- Predictive machine learning

The system produces:

- Freshness score
- Ripeness estimate
- Visible defect detection
- Spoilage-risk estimate
- Remaining shelf-life estimate
- Batch priority
- Recommended action
- Estimated potential waste reduction

---

# 3. Core Concept

Instead of simply asking:

> "Is this fruit fresh?"

Taza asks:

> **"How fresh is this produce, how long is it likely to remain usable, what is its deterioration risk, and what should we do with it?"**
